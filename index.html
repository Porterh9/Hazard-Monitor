Monitor address

Your monitor's address on your home network. This page only reaches it from a phone or computer on that same WiFi.

Where do I find it?

If you've just connected the monitor to WiFi, it's on the screen in front of you, in large type.

Otherwise tap the gear at the top-left of the monitor's screen and read the number next to Address.

On an iPhone, Mac, or recent Windows PC you can usually type hazard.local instead.

How do I look these up?
  1. Open Google Maps and find your home.
  2. Press and hold on it (or right-click on a computer).
  3. Two numbers appear. The first is Latitude, the second Longitude.
  4. Across the Americas the second one is negative. Keep the minus sign.

This is not a warning system

The Hazard Monitor is for situational awareness. It polls public feeds every few minutes and can be late, stale, or offline exactly when weather is worst. Never let it stand in for an official alert: keep NOAA Weather Radio, Wireless Emergency Alerts on your phone, and your local emergency management as the things you actually act on.

Your monitor reaches these services directly from your own network. BenchLabs has no account, no cloud service, and receives nothing from your device. This page talks only to your monitor and to the US Census geocoder when you look up a ZIP.

Alerts and forecasts from the NOAA / National Weather Service. Earthquakes from the US Geological Survey. Tropical cyclones from the National Hurricane Center. ZIP lookup and map boundaries from the US Census Bureau. All US Government works in the public domain.
